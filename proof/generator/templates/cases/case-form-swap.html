A form swap cut paid leads in half. Finding the cause.

Client work, 2026. Client not named

Problem
A B2B software client moved its site forms to a new marketing platform. Paid leads fell by about 60% from one day to the next.
What I did
Worked the drop from the ad click to the form until one cause was left: a submit button nobody could reach.
Result
The fix went out on every landing page. Recovery figures to come

I ran the client's paid search and its conversion tracking. The client's web lead owned the forms. We found and fixed this together.

How I found it

In this order

  1. Date the drop

    It started the day the forms changed. Nothing else changed that day.

  2. Rule out the traffic

    Clicks were flat. The loss was after the click.

  3. Split two problems

    Fewer leads, and some leads arriving with no source. Different causes.

  4. Use the page as a visitor

    Fill the form in on a laptop, a phone, with and without an ad blocker.

  5. Find it

    The submit button was out of reach.

The form sat inside a fixed-size block on each landing page. A script hid most fields until a visitor typed an email, then revealed the rest. The extra fields pushed the submit button below the bottom edge of the block, where it could not be seen or clicked.

What I chose, and what I turned down

ChoseStart from the date the drop began.

Turned downStarting from bids and budgets.

WhyA drop that begins in one day has one cause. Bids drift.

ChoseCompare the rate of leads per click, before and after.

Turned downComparing lead counts.

WhyCounts move with traffic. The rate isolates the page.

ChoseA direct form embed with no tag manager in the path.

Turned downKeeping the form injected by a tag.

WhyAd blockers were stopping the tag. The direct embed also loaded faster.

What went wrong, and what I do now

My first read blamed one ad platform.Its total had fallen most. Search leads had in fact fallen by about half on both. The other platform was cushioned by a different campaign type.
FixI compare like with like before naming a cause: the same campaign type on each platform.
The first fix had to be applied page by page.The block the form sat in has a different ID on every page.
FixThe direct embed replaced it, so there is no per-page patch to forget.
I changed every live page when the ask was one test page.
Next timeOne test page first, even when the fix looks obvious.
