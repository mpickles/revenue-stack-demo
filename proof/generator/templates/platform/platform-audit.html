How a sweep runs

Every client, in this order

  1. 1

    It reads the ad accounts and the tag containers, and crawls the site.

  2. 2

    It checks in a fixed order: account settings, conversion actions, the tag container, consent, click IDs, then whether lead counts agree within 10%.

  3. 3

    It rolls findings up to one row per client per rule.

  4. 4

    A person marks each row. Form and conversion tests only run when someone starts them.

What I chose, and what I turned down

ChoseThe auditor never publishes. A fix arrives as an import file plus written steps.

Turned downLetting the system publish tag changes.

Why, and what it costA wrong publish breaks a client's tracking. The cost: a person does the last step by hand.

ChoseA person marks each finding: not relevant, or checked and working.

Turned downShowing every finding to everyone, every time.

Why, and what it costNot every finding matters to every client. The list gets shorter each run.

ChoseEvidence first. Paused tags are skipped.

Turned downFlagging on a rule alone.

Why, and what it costCalling something broken needs proof a person can open.

2 more choices

Turned downRunning them on a schedule.

Why, and what it costThey touch a client's live site. That should never be a surprise.

ChoseA page that answers three questions in order: is it working, how does it connect, what is missing.

Turned downA map of every tag.

Why, and what it costThe map looked clever and nobody could use it.
