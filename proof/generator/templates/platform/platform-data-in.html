Problem
The numbers lived in five tools, and someone downloaded them by hand.
What I built
Scheduled loads into one warehouse, and a script that captures account changes with no developer token.
Result
Every account loads on a schedule. Account changes are on record, day by day.

I built every load myself: scheduled loads from the ad platforms, scripts for what those miss, and one control sheet that says which client gets what.

Where the cost went

Three things read the warehouse every day. I cut all three.

How I cut each one

  1. Morning build

    It stopped rebuilding all of history. Now it rebuilds recent days only, looks back 35 days for late conversions, and does a full refresh on Sundays.

  2. Screens in the app

    Screens read saved copies made after each build. The warehouse is only asked live for a drill-down, with a cap.

  3. Scripts outside the build

    Every script query is capped at 1 GB. Daily caps per project and per person refuse anything past the line.

Change history, with no developer token

A script inside the ad accounts stores what changed each day. A report can then show a change next to what happened after it.

How the capture works

  1. 1

    Each morning a script in the manager account reads the last 3 days of changes for every client account.

  2. 2

    It pages past the 10,000-row limit, where the official route silently drops the rest.

  3. 3

    The build removes duplicates into one table of changes.

  4. 4

    It keeps only whether we, the client or automation made the change. Never a name.

Why it is built the way it is

BecauseThe official route needs a developer token I did not have.
SoThe capture runs as a script inside the ad accounts.

No token needed, and it pages past the row cap the official route has.

BecauseA budget alert only sends an email.
SoHard quotas are the limit.

A quota refuses the query. An alert tells you after the money is gone.

BecauseChanging a client setting used to need a redeploy.
SoSettings live in one control sheet.

It syncs to the warehouse every morning, before anything rebuilds.

What I chose, and what I turned down

ChoseFree pipes: the ad platform's own free transfer, plus scripts that post the rest.

Turned downPaid connectors, at $100 to over $1,000 a month.

Why, and what it costNo budget for tools. The cost: one platform only arrives at campaign level.

ChoseClient settings in a sheet that syncs all or nothing.

Turned downSettings kept in code.

Why, and what it costAnyone on the team can change a setting. The cost: one note 19 characters too long once blocked a whole sync.

ChoseOne writer per table. The sheet owns it or the app does. Never both.

Turned downA two-way sync.

Why, and what it costTwo writers means two versions of the truth. The cost: moving tables over to the app took longer than planned.

ChoseOne dispatcher as the only clock for every job.

Turned downA scheduled job per task.

Why, and what it costThe free tier allows three scheduled jobs. One clock is also one place to look when something does not run.

What broke, and what I did about it

The first production build had zero rows.

Account IDs were written with dashes in one place and without in another.

FixOne format everywhere.
My first cost fix missed its target.

I assumed a few heavy tables were the problem. The cost was hundreds of small tables, each billed a minimum read.

FixI now measure the read per table before cutting anything.

What I learned, and what I would do differently

The ad platform only keeps 30 days of change history. Every day before the script ran is gone for good.
Next timeStart capturing history on day one, before anything else is built.
Client settings were baked into the deploy. I forgot one redeploy, and production rebuilt from stale settings every morning for a month.
Next timeNever keep a setting where changing it needs a deploy. The control sheet fixed this.
My first cost fix was a guess, and it missed.
Next timeMeasure the read per table first. Then cut.
